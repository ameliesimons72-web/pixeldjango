Since 1886

A north London club with a world-wide following.

Arsenal Football Club was founded in 1886 by workers at the Royal Arsenal in Woolwich. The club moved north of the river to Highbury in 1913, beginning a new chapter in north London. Today, supporters around the world know the team as Arsenal or the Gunners.

A tradition of expressive football, memorable teams, and strong community ties has shaped the club's identity across generations. Its red-and-white colours are recognised by fans on matchdays and far beyond London.

A landmark season

The Invincibles

In 2003–04, Arsenal completed the 38-match Premier League season unbeaten, an achievement that remains one of English football's defining campaigns.

Home of the Gunners

Emirates Stadium

Opened in 2006 in Holloway, north London, the stadium is Arsenal's home ground and a central gathering place for supporters on matchday.

Honours

A record in the cup

Arsenal's men's first team has won 14 FA Cups, more than any other club, alongside league titles and a long history of domestic and European competition.

More than football

Rooted in the community

Arsenal's story includes the people and neighbourhoods around it. The club's community work supports young people, education, wellbeing, and inclusion through the Arsenal Foundation and local programmes. Generations of players, staff, and supporters have helped make the club's identity bigger than the result on the pitch.
